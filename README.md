# Orion Global — Cross-Border Cyber Incident Response Playbook

**Academic cyber law and incident-response project | July 2026 | Jack W. Danford Jr.**

## Overview

Created a visual incident-response playbook for a **fictional ransomware and double-extortion incident** affecting Orion Global's operations and data across Texas, the United States, the European Union, and Japan.

The playbook combines technical response priorities, legal-notification decision paths, evidence preservation, and cross-functional accountability. Its organizing mission is to **contain the attack, preserve evidence, track applicable legal deadlines, coordinate communications, and restore safely**.

**[View the 12-page visual playbook (PDF)](Orion_Global_Incident_Response_Playbook.pdf)**

## What's inside

The PDF includes a cover, **eight core visual response pages**, a supplemental legal-deadline verification page, and two pages of references.

| Area | Academic deliverable |
| --- | --- |
| Situation report | Incident timeline, system impact map, and response goals |
| Global triage | Decision map for affected personal data, systems, jurisdictions, and sector-specific rules |
| Texas breach reporting | Conditional resident and Attorney General notification decision path |
| U.S. federal review | Conditional HIPAA, FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, SEC, CIRCIA, and sanctions questions |
| European Union | GDPR breach reporting and potentially applicable NIS2 incident-reporting tracks |
| Japan | APPI/PPC reporting and notice decision map |
| Cross-border coordination | One incident command structure with regional legal specialists and documented decisions |
| Evidence preservation | Proposed collection windows, legal holds, chain of custody, secure storage, and ownership |

## Important qualifications

- This work is a **fictional course scenario**, not an actual client engagement or a live incident.
- The legal analysis and deadlines were researched for a **July 2026 academic submission**; law and agency guidance can change. This project is **not current legal advice**.
- Legal duties are conditional on facts such as entity classification, type of data, affected individuals, and applicable jurisdictions.
- No real systems were examined, notifications sent, forensic collections performed, or legal determinations issued.
- The project documents an academic approach and is not a claim of paid cybersecurity, legal, or GRC employment.
- The original PDF includes source references and acknowledges that actual decisions require qualified legal review.
